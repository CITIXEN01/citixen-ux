Wis. Stat. § 893.80 Statutory Defense

  • Automated 120-Day Notice Log: Every submitted report is logged to a SHA-256 hash-chained ledger to preserve tamper-evident timeline evidence under Wis. Stat. § 893.80.SHA-256 is a standard cryptographic hash function (NIST FIPS 180-4). It turns any record into a fixed 64-character fingerprint; change a single character and the fingerprint changes completely.

    In a hash chain, each entry’s fingerprint includes the one before it, so editing an old entry breaks every entry after it. That makes changes detectable (tamper-evident), not impossible, and it does not show who made them.

Wis. Stat. § 893.80 Brief

Notice-of-claim rules for claims against Wisconsin towns, villages, cities and counties, and what CITIXEN UX does and does not do about them.

1 · The notice-of-claim rule

  • Before suing a village, a person generally must serve written notice of the circumstances of the claim within 120 days of the event, signed by the claimant, agent or attorney.
  • The statute also lets a claim proceed when the Village had actual notice of the claim and was not prejudiced by the lack of written notice.
  • The claimant must also present the claim, with an address and an itemized statement of the relief sought, to the Village for it to allow or disallow.

2 · After a claim is presented

  • A disallowance is served by registered or certified mail. A suit must be brought within 6 months of that notice.
  • If the Village does not act within 120 days of presentation, that counts as a disallowance.

3 · Limits on recovery

  • Damages against a political subdivision and its officials are capped (generally $50,000), and punitive damages are not recoverable.
  • The statute bars suits for intentional torts of officers and employees, and for legislative, quasi-legislative, judicial or quasi-judicial acts.

4 · What CITIXEN UX adds

  • Each notice is recorded in a SHA-256 hash-chained log, so the Village can show when a report was submitted and when it was resolved.
  • Because each entry’s fingerprint includes the one before it, a later edit to any entry is detectable (tamper-evident).

5 · What it does not do

  • It is a record-keeping tool, not legal advice. It does not itself confer immunity or establish a statutory defense.
  • It does not serve or receive statutory notices for the Village, decide whether a claim is valid, or show who made an edit.
